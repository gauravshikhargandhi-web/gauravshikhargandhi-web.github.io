Formula Student: Funding the Aero

Our team's first full aerodynamics package was about to be cut for budget, so I ran the team's first fundraising campaign and beat the target by 25% in twenty days.

Year2017
RoleHead of aerodynamics and fundraising
ContextFormula Student, BITS Pilani
TeamStudent racing team
20days to raise the money
25%over the fundraising target
40%of the team's budget raised
1stfull aero package in team history

01Problem

We were attempting full aerodynamics for the first time, and we'd already put a lot of time into the design. Then the team ran critically short of money. The car could race without aero, so aero was the obvious thing to cut, and I didn't want a fight over money to split the team.

02What I did

  1. I recommended the team spend the budget on the systems the car needed to race: powertrain, electronics and the core build. It was my own project losing out, but a half-built car would have been worse.
  2. I proposed an experiment to the aero sub-team: the first fundraising campaign in the team's history, with a hard limit of twenty days. If we missed the target, we'd drop aero and move on.
  3. I led the outreach myself, to alumni, friends and family, with direct asks. When some sponsors offered parts and services instead of cash, I put more effort into those, because they turn into relationships that come back every year.

03Outcome

We raised 25% more than the target in twenty days. That paid for the aero program and for electronics projects we'd written off at the start of the year. The in-kind sponsors came back year after year, and the team now runs the campaign every season, long after I graduated.

04What I took from it

Aim high, and know when to step back for the team. Resourcefulness can get you what the budget can't. And the thing you build to solve a problem, here a fundraising playbook, can outlast the problem itself.
