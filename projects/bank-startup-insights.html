Startup Banking Insights

Four AI-powered features that help a global bank's Series A to C clients make finance decisions. Capstone project.

01Problem

How might a bank's digital platform give maturing startups intelligent, personalized data experiences, tailored to each user's role, company stage and industry? Startups between Series A and C hit an inflection point. The question shifts from "do we have enough cash?" to "how do we scale and win?" That is when a bank's help matters most, and when generic dashboards stop being useful.

02How I thought about it

We used the double diamond: discover, define, develop, deliver. After interviews with founders, CFOs and analysts, we built personas around roles, not company size. The financial analyst became our anchor: she needs a strategic partner, not a ledger.

  1. Stage: what changes between survival, growth and scaling.
  2. Moments: month-end close, payroll and tax, board reporting, budget planning.
  3. Jobs to be done: the progress a user is trying to make in each moment.
  4. Signals: which data, surfaced when, would change a decision.

That led to five How Might We questions on international expansion, capital management, data integration, operations and risk.

03What we built

  • Founder Connect: opens up the bank's network of investors, partners and peers to founders.
  • Growth Expansion Dashboard: turns stage, balance sheet strength and policy into expansion and capital recommendations.
  • Vendor Intelligence: flags vendor risk and payment optimization from transaction data.
  • Morning briefing: a daily digest that translates yesterday's transactions into board-ready insight.

I built the working prototype as an interactive app with Claude, so testers clicked through real flows instead of static slides.

04What testing showed

We tested each feature with finance practitioners at a construction firm, a PE-backed consumer goods company and a fintech. They rated clarity, signal credibility, trust and adoption. The morning briefing scored 5 out of 5 on clarity and credibility. Vendor Intelligence scored 5 out of 5 on trust. One clear finding: in banking, stability beats cool features.
