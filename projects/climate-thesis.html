Climate Venture Thesis

An investment thesis for a new early-stage fund that carries physical climate companies across the valley of death.

01Problem

For most climate technology the science works. The hard part is funding the jump from a lab bench to a first factory. That gap, the valley of death, is where good ideas quietly die, and where the right early investor changes everything.

One of the most active early-stage investors in the country asked our team a simple question: what does a new fund look like that is designed to carry early-stage climate companies across that gap?

02How we approached it

  1. Benchmarked the field: how the best climate and deep-tech funds pick their companies.
  2. Talked to the experts: 20+ founders, investors and operators who live in this world.
  3. Mapped the market: sized the opportunity and looked for the openings others were missing.

03What we found

Greener only wins if it is better. The companies worth backing are cheaper, faster, better and cleaner. The climate upside comes free with a great product.

The timing matters too. AI is pushing energy demand up faster than anything in recent memory. Rising costs and new rules are forcing change. Supply chains and energy security now drive adoption. And real hardware know-how is something software alone can't copy.

04The thesis

We focused on three areas where physical technology and AI meet:

  • Energy transition: grid intelligence and resilience, the built environment, adaptation.
  • Materials: critical minerals, textiles and apparel, specialty chemicals.
  • Waste: sorting and recycling, waste-to-value, reverse logistics.

Every company has to pass three tests: customer validation, novel technology and strategic bankability. Most early investors stop at the founder and the technology. We also ask whether the business can stand on its own years from now, and how it gets to project finance.

05Why it matters to me

I spent five years on the hard part of hardware: getting a product out of the lab and into a plant. This thesis is the same problem seen from the investor's side, and it's the lens I now use on every climate startup I meet.
