CloudCycle: Going Electric

Winner, Kellogg Design Challenge. A system that helps small landscapers switch from gas to electric power equipment.

01Problem

How might a power-equipment maker drive broader uptake of electric equipment among its key customers? Battery tools are getting better and cities are restricting gas equipment, but the people who use mowers and blowers all day are slow to switch.

02How I thought about it

We talked to 16 people: homeowners, in-store shoppers at home-improvement stores and landscapers online. We mapped competitors on usability versus maintenance and performance. The commercial user stood out, so we focused on one persona: Jeff, a small landscaping business owner.

  • Charging: "Finding places to charge equipment... some houses don't want you to charge there."
  • Price: battery-powered equipment is expensive up front.
  • Maintenance: about 5% of work hours go to fixing gas tools, which pushes him toward electric.

As an engineer, I saw it as a systems problem, not a product problem. Landscapers invest to cut downtime, and they trust a single ecosystem once it works.

03What we built

A full electric tool system for residential landscapers, launched in three horizons:

  1. Quick Start (year 1): a fuel-versus-battery savings calculator, two batteries with every purchase and a trial subscription.
  2. Power Pulse (years 1 to 2): an app for battery status and predictive charging reminders, with smart chargers that chime when a pack is ready.
  3. Charge-Up Trailer (2+ years): a mobile trailer with built-in charging, tool storage and battery backup, so a crew never depends on a client's outlet.

04Testing and refining

  • Users had app fatigue, so we added physical battery reminders.
  • They wanted proof before paying, so we added try-before-you-buy.
  • Trust in the brand mattered more than novelty: "I want to buy from a tool company I know, not some startup."
